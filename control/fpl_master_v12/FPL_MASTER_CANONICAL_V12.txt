20N. PAIRWISE vs MULTI-PLAYER CHAIN OWNERSHIP / ANTI-DOUBLE-COUNT
A. PAIRWISE EDGE OWNER: a direct source->target edge, for example B->C, owns
the conditional target effect of B availability relative to C baseline after
existing P1.1 P(start) marginalization. That direct contribution is applied
once in the pairwise runtime path.
B. MULTI-PLAYER CHAIN OWNER: a chain such as A->B->C owns ONLY the
higher-order/incremental upstream interaction that remains after the direct
terminal B->C contribution already owned by pairwise has been removed. Chain
effect MUST NOT equal a second multiplication of every raw edge effect.
C. A terminal edge may be used as evidence and as the confidence/continuity
bridge that makes a chain valid, but its direct target contribution may not be
multiplied twice. Runtime decomposition must expose raw chain contribution,
overlapping pairwise contribution, overlap edge IDs, incremental/residual chain
contribution, incremental confidence and effective chain multiplier.
D. Anti-double-count is performed before the existing final contextual bound.
The global combined multiplier cap is only a safety bound and MUST NOT be
treated as an anti-double-count mechanism.
E. Positive and negative dependencies use the same neutral-relative residual
semantics. If pairwise=1.10 and raw joint chain=1.16, incremental chain is
approximately 1.16/1.10. If pairwise=0.92 and raw joint chain=0.86,
incremental chain is approximately 0.86/0.92.
F. P1.1 availability remains authoritative. The incremental chain interaction
is gated by the existing chain-intact probability. If a required middle node
has P(start)=0, the higher-order residual approaches neutral while the pairwise
